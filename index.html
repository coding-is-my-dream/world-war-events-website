Major events of World War I (WWI)

1914

June 28: Austro-Hungarian Archduke Franz Ferdinand and his wife Sophie Chotek are assassinated by Bosnian Serb nationalist Gavrilo Princip. Austria suspects Serbia is responsible.

July 28: Austria-Hungary declare a war on Serbia.

August: Germany declares war on Russia, France and Belgium. Britain declares war on Germany. Austria-Hungary declares war on Russia. France and Britain declares war on Austria-Hungary. Japan declares war on Germany. Austria-Hungary declares war on Belgium. The US declares their neutrality.

September 4: Germany invades Belgium but is stopped at the First Battle of the Marne. The Schlieffen Plan fails.

October 31: First Battle of Ypres attempting to outflank each other, Allied and German troops were unable to win a decisive victory, leading to the onset of trench warfare.

November 5: Britain and France declare war on the Ottoman Empire

1915

January 10: German zeppelin raids on Great Britain begin, bringing the war home to British civilians.

February 4: Germany initiates a policy of unrestricted submarine warfare, whereby all merchant ships including those of neutral countries, would be subject to attack.

April 22 to May 25: Second Battle of Ypres: Germans launch the first successful gas attack in history. By the end of the war both Allied and Central Powers will have used chemical weapons.

April 25: British and French troops including Australians and New Zealanders (ANZAC) land on the Gallipoli peninsula in the Ottoman Empire.

May 7: German U-boat torpedoes the Lusitania, a British passenger liner killing 128 Americans.

May 23: Italy declares war on Austria-Hungary, entering World War I on the side of the Allies.

September 28: Allied troops move through Mesopotamia to capture Baghdad from the Ottomans.

October 6: A combined force of Austro-Hungarians, Germans and later Bulgarians invade Serbia. After weeks of stubborn fighting, the Serbian Army was forced to retreat through Montenegro and Albania.

October 7 to 12: British Red cross nurse Edith Cavell who secretly helped hundreds of British, French and Belgian soldiers escape the Germans is arrested for espionage and executed in Brussels

1916

February 21: Battle of Verdun, Hoping to “bleed France white” the German Army launches a major offensive against the symbolic fortress of Verdun. Fighting did not end until December.

April 22: ~20k French women and girls are deported from industrial cities like Lille to perform forced agricultural work in other parts of occupied France.

May 16: Great Britain and France secretly sign the Sykes-Picot Agreement. Assuming they will defeat the Ottoman Empire, they divide the Middle East. France claims Syria and Lebanon. Britain claims Jordan, Iraq, the Gulf states and the Palestine Mandate.

May 31: Battle of Jutland, In the largest naval battle of the war, Britain’s Royal Navy Grand Fleet and the German Navy’s High Seas Fleet fought to a draw, though both sides claimed victory.

June 5: Arab nationalists revolt against Ottoman rule in the “Great Arab Rising.”

July 1: Battle of the Somme, After a week of artillery bombardment, Allied troops launch an offensive meant to divert German troops from Verdun. The British suffered ~50k casualties on the first day and fighting continued until November.

1917

February 3: After Germany resumes its campaign of unrestricted submarine warfare, the US severs diplomatic relations with Germany

March 1: British intelligence intercepts the Zimmermann Telegram, a secret communication from Germany proposing an alliance with Mexico should the US enter World War I.

April 1: Germany sinks the SS Aztec a US cargo ship bound for France.

April 2: US President Woodrow Wilson delivers a war message to Congress, famously stating “the world must be made safe for democracy.”

April 6: Following passage of the war resolution by the Senate and House, the US is officially at war with the German Empire.

April 16: Having traveled on a sealed train from Switzerland, Vladimir Lenin returns to Petrograd (St. Petersburg) after a decade of exile to personally lead the Russian Revolution.

May 18: The US Congress authorizes the Selective Service Act, initiating the first military draft since the Civil War.

May 25: General John J. Pershing is selected as commander of the American Expeditionary Forces.

June 15: Congress passes the Espionage Act making it a crime for any person to convey information intended to interfere with the prosecution of the war effort or to promote the success of the country’s enemies.

July 31: Third Battle of Ypres (Passchendaele), Allied troops largely those from the British Empire, launch an attack to seize key ridges near Ypres. They achieve victory, but only after months of fighting in horrific conditions and sustaining heavy casualties.

November 2: Britain issues the Balfour Declaration which is a statement of support for the establishment of a Jewish nation in Palestine.

November 7: Vladimir Lenin and the Bolsheviks assume complete control over the new Soviet Russian state.

December 6: A French munitions ship collides with a Belgian relief ship resulting in ~11k casualties

December 9: The British capture Jerusalem from the Ottomans.

1918

January 8: US President Woodrow Wilson outlines his Fourteen Points for peace.

March 3: Russia and Germany sign the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk officially removing Russia from World War I.

March 8: Camp Funston at Fort Riley, Kansas makes the first report of influenza. The disease spreads overseas to the Western Front. Over the next year this “Spanish Influenza" kills 20 million worldwide.

May 28: Battle of Cantigny, In its first major battle of World War I American troops captured the town of Cantigny, depriving the Germans of an important observation point

July 17: Bolsheviks murder the former czar of Russia Nicholas II and his family.

July 18 to August 6: Aisne-Marne Offensive marks a major turning point in the fighting on the Western Front. Two days after its conclusion, the British attack at Amiens is called the “Black Day of the German Army.”

September 12 to 15: Battle of Saint-Mihiel, First major offensive operation by General John J. Pershing’s American Expeditionary Forces.

September 26: Meuse-Argonne Offensive, After a short artillery bombardment American and French troops advance toward German positions in the Argonne Forest and along the Meuse River. The largest offensive in US history which played a major role in bringing about an end to the war.

October 24 to November 3: Battle of Vittorio Veneto, Austro-Hungarian forces are severely defeated by the Italian Army ending the war on the Italian Front and ushering in the final dissolution of the Austro-Hungarian Empire.

November 9: German Kaiser Wilhelm II abdicates and flees to Holland. German Republic (later the Weimar Republic) proclaimed.

November 11: Having been given 72 hours to agree to Allied demands, Germany signs the armistice. Supreme Allied Commander Marshal Ferdinand Foch orders that all hostilities on the Western Front cease at 11 AM Paris time.

December 1: Allied troops move into Germany and begin occupation. Yugoslavia a kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes is proclaimed an independent state.

1919

June 28 : The Treaty of Versailles is signed, formally concluding peace, redrawing the map of Europe, and placing heavy reparations and war guilt on Germany
